Museum History

Chronicles of a world transformed by the ever-growing sea

The Convergence Event

The modern world emerged from a phenomenon now referred to as the Convergence Event, a multi-dimensional pinpoint where numerous anomalies began to overlap and stabilize. Physical laws bent, planetary scale expanded, and the oceans began a relentless rise. What was once considered catastrophic flooding became the planet's defining condition. The sea did not merely consume land; it rewrote it.

Geological records indicate that the planet's mass increased without a proportional rise in gravity, suggesting spatial distortion rather than material accumulation. As coastlines vanished, new aquatic continents formed beneath the surface, reshaping migration, settlement, and survival.

Aquatic Transformation

The spread of water acted as an infectious environmental force, altering the biology of nearly all lifeforms. Plants developed fluid-retentive tissues and translucent membranes. Terrestrial animals adapted gills, webbing, and buoyant skeletal structures. Even inland ecosystems began to mirror oceanic aesthetics, lush with flowing fronds and bioluminescent growth.

These adaptations occurred far beyond any shoreline, reinforcing the understanding that the sea is no longer a location, but a planetary condition. Life did not retreat from the water; it became it.

Peoples of the Tides

Humans now coexist with fish-like and monstrous humanoids whose origins remain partially unexplained. Some exhibit evolutionary traits consistent with long-term aquatic adaptation, while others appear to have emerged directly from anomaly exposure. Many societies are richly hybrid, blending human and non-human customs, labor, and belief systems.

Beyond these communities exist beings that defy categorization entirely. Fragmentary records describe entities without stable form, language, or anatomy, acknowledged but rarely studied due to ethical and safety concerns.

Modern Governance and Anomaly Oversight

Despite the strangeness of the world, daily life retains familiar structures. Cities persist, trade flows, and cultural exchange thrives across five major nations scattered across the expanded globe. Australia, uniquely suited to maritime survival even before the Convergence, emerged as a central nautical stronghold and refuge.

Beneath public governance exists a classified, multinational branch dedicated to monitoring anomalies. Its mandate is prevention, research, and containment, operating under the belief that anomalies may arise with benevolent or malicious intent. Much of what is known today survives because of their quiet intervention.
